"Public domain" sounds like a single switch, and it is not. It is a set of country-by-country
rules about when a work stops being controlled by anyone, plus a separate question about the
particular file you are downloading.

## The author's text and the edition

The author's text and the edition of that text are two different things. A novel from 1890 is
public domain; the 2019 annotated edition of it may still be under copyright, because the
introduction, notes and typography are new work. This is why a site can legally offer last
year's edition of a very old book only if the publisher gave permission — and why a download
site offering scanned commercial editions is usually not doing what it claims.

## The three checks

A free library that means to stay legal checks three things for every book:

1. **The work.** Is it public domain in the country the site serves? In China the general rule
   is the author's life plus fifty years; in the United States, works published before 1930
   are clear as of 2025. The two rules do not always agree.
2. **The edition.** Was this particular file produced by a source that has the right to
   publish it — Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks, a university press, the author?
3. **The translation.** A translation has its own copyright, held by the translator. A
   nineteenth-century Russian novel is public domain; a 1990 translation of it is not.

## Why linking is not hosting

A link to a publisher's own download page is not the same act as copying the file onto a
server. The first is a recommendation; the second is distribution, and distribution is what
copyright law is about. A site that links to Standard Ebooks is doing something different
from a site that mirrors a shadow library, even if both have the same red cover on screen.

## What this means for the shelf here

Every free book on this site is public domain, and the file comes from the publisher named on
the book page. Nothing here is a scan of a commercial edition; nothing is behind an email
wall; nothing is a mirror. Where a book is still in copyright, the page goes to a licensed
store and no file is offered at all.

That is a smaller library than a shadow one, and it is the only kind that can stay online.
