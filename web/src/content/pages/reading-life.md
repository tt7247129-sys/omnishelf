# Reading Life

Reading is treated as a mental activity and it is also a physical one. Where you sit, how
the light falls, whether the page stays open when you let go of it — these decide whether
a twenty-minute reading session happens at all, more reliably than motivation does.

This column is not a shopping list, and it is suspicious of the genre. A reading lamp is
not interesting because it is warm-white at 3000K; it is interesting because a
directional light lets one person read in a room where another person is asleep, which is
the difference between reading at night and not reading at night. A book stand is not
interesting because it is adjustable; it is interesting because it removes the hand that
was holding the page open, and that hand was doing the only job that made one-handed
reading impossible.

Each piece starts from an obstacle of that kind — a specific failure of a specific
evening — and works back to the smallest configuration that removes it. Products appear
as examples of a shape, with their limits stated, not as recommendations to buy.

The test for every suggestion here is the same: would this still be in use in six months,
or is it the thing you bought once and moved to a drawer?
