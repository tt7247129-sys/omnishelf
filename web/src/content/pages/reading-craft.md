# Reading Craft

Most advice about reading describes the act from a distance: read more, read actively,
take notes. None of it survives the first specific question — what do you do when the
table of contents is a scrollable column instead of a page you can flip to, or when the
margin you were going to annotate does not exist because the text reflows?

This column takes the classical methods seriously and then tests them against a device.
Mortimer Adler's four levels of reading were written for printed books with fixed pages,
and three of the four behave differently when the page is a viewport. The SQ3R study
method assumes you can survey a chapter by looking at its headings on a spread; on a
phone, "survey" means scrolling through a document whose structural cues have been
flattened. Annotation, as a discipline, depends on the physical constraint of a margin
that holds four words — and that constraint is what makes a marginal note short enough
to be useful.

Each piece here starts from one of those collisions and works outward: what the method
was for, what the device breaks, and what is left that still works. The recommendations
are specific to a screen size and a file format because that is the only level at which
advice is testable.

If you want the engineering behind the reader, that lives in
[Engineering notes](/notes/). This column is about the reading, not the software.
