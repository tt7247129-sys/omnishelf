A shelf of unread books is not a plan; it is a mood. The digital version is worse, because
there is no physical limit and no guilt at the door: a wish list can grow for years without
anyone having to find room for it.

## The limit is the feature

A shelf with a limit forces a decision, and decisions are what a reading life is made of. Pick
a number — six, ten, whatever the month can hold — and treat the shelf as full until a book
leaves it.

## The queue has a length

The queue (what you will read next, in order) should hold two or three books, not twenty.
Twenty is a wish list pretending to be a plan. A short queue keeps the next choice already
made, which is the only reason to have one.

## The two-page rule

If a book has not given you a reason to continue by page two — a sentence, a question, a
voice — it is allowed to leave. If it has, give it eighty pages before deciding again. This
rule alone clears most of a stuck shelf.

## Finishing is not a virtue

The number of books finished is a bad metric: it rewards short books and punishes difficult
ones, and it makes abandoning a book feel like failure. A better one: how many books you can
say something about a month later.

## Where the shelf lives

Keeping the shelf in the same app that reads the books is a small thing with a large effect:
the unread book is one tap from the first page, and the finished book is one tap from its
highlights. A shelf in a separate app is a shelf you visit, not one you use.

## The point

Reading is not a collection. A shelf that ends, a queue that is short and a rule for
quitting are not restrictions on reading; they are what makes room for it.
